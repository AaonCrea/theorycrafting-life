My core belief is simple: **a rising tide lifts all boats.** Leadership is less about control and more about building an honest current that helps people move forward together.

## Leadership is a current

People do better work when they understand the standard, know where they stand, and trust that the system is not working against them. A leader’s job is to make that current easier to enter and easier to follow.

That means setting clear expectations, communicating changes early, and paying attention to the friction that keeps good people from doing good work.

## What that looks like

- **Make the work visible.** People should be able to see priorities, constraints, and the next useful action.
- **Keep accountability human.** Standards matter, but correction should leave room for context, learning, and repair.
- **Build systems that carry people.** A process should reduce confusion and distribute effort — not depend on one person constantly rescuing it.

## The test

The test is not whether a team can survive a heroic push. It is whether the team can perform well on an ordinary day, with clear information and a reasonable amount of energy left at the end.

That is the kind of leadership I want to practice: practical, honest, and strong enough to help everyone rise.
