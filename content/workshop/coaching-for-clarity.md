Good coaching does not need to be dramatic. It is a repeatable loop that helps people understand the work, recognize what is happening, and practice the next useful step.

## Start with the standard

Before giving feedback, make sure the destination is visible. People need to know what good work looks like, why it matters, and which part of the standard matters most right now.

Clear expectations make coaching more useful because the conversation can stay focused on the work instead of becoming a guessing game about intent.

## Look at the work

Observation is more useful than assumption. Watch the process, listen to the explanation, and notice where the work becomes easier or harder than it should be.

The goal is not to collect mistakes. It is to find the point where a small amount of clarity, practice, or support can improve the whole loop.

## Make feedback specific

Useful feedback connects three things:

- what happened;
- why it matters; and
- what to try next.

That keeps the conversation concrete. It also gives the person something they can act on instead of a label they have to carry.

## Practice the next step

Coaching becomes real when there is room to try again. The next attempt does not need to be perfect. It needs to be visible enough to learn from.

Small practice turns an abstract expectation into a usable habit. Over time, that is how confidence grows: not through one speech, but through a series of understandable next steps.

## Close the loop

Follow-up is part of the coaching, not an optional extra. Check what changed, recognize progress, and adjust the next step when new information appears.

The best coaching loop leaves the work clearer than it found it and leaves the person better equipped for the next round.
