A year is a long time to leave a site quiet. It gives you enough distance to see what was useful, what had become clutter, and which ideas were still worth carrying forward.

Theorycrafting.life started as a place to collect ideas about leadership, systems, creativity, and self-improvement. The first version did its job, but it had become more of a snapshot than a working notebook.

So I rebuilt the foundation before adding more weight to it.

## Build the foundation first

The first practical step was a reusable contact-card system. Business cards should be simple: a name, a role, a way to call, a way to write, directions, and a reliable way to save the contact.

Each profile now has its own page and vCard, while the shared layout keeps the implementation small. Public visibility is intentional too: my card is linked from the main site, while other hosted profiles can remain available by direct link without appearing in navigation, feeds, or search results.

## Refine the forge

The visual refresh keeps the dark, forge-inspired character but gives it more room to breathe. The new shell has a clearer navigation system, a stronger homepage, shorter section introductions, and article layouts that make the writing easier to scan.

The About page needed the most work. It had good material, but too many overlapping explanations. I reduced it to a clearer through-line: build the work, understand the system, and improve the conditions around people.

## Keep the system light

The site still runs on Hugo and Cloudflare Pages. There is no database, account system, subscription layer, unnecessary tracking, or heavy front-end framework behind it.

That simplicity matters. A personal site should be easy to change when the person changes. It should load quickly, remain readable, and make the next improvement feel approachable instead of expensive.

## What comes next

The rebuild is not a finish line. It is a better starting point for new notes, practical tools, project logs, and creative experiments.

I want this site to stay useful rather than merely look complete. That means writing when there is something worth keeping, testing ideas in the open, and refining the system whenever it starts getting in the way.

Everything can be refined — systems, stories, and self.
